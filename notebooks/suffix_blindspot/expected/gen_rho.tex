Across 5 seeds with independently-drawn benign masks per run: CatBoost $\rho_{between} = 0.968\pm0.018$ vs.\ within-protocol re-seeding baselines $0.973$ (random) and $0.967$ (phishing-temporal); XGBoost $\rho_{between} = 0.968\pm0.016$ vs.\ within-protocol re-seeding baselines $0.958$ (random) and $0.984$ (phishing-temporal). Read against their own baselines, the two families resolve differently. For CatBoost the between-protocol correlation lies inside the re-seeding band, so switching the protocol perturbs the reliance ranking no more than re-training under the same protocol does. For XGBoost ($0.968$ against its phishing-temporal baseline of $0.984 \pm 0.007$) it falls short of the baseline by more than the baseline's own seed-to-seed standard deviation. Each verdict compares a family with its own null; the difference between the families is not itself tested, and their between-protocol correlations are the same to three decimals. One caveat the headline number hides: 5 of the 21 features (\texttt{is\_ip}, \texttt{eq\_cnt}, \texttt{qm\_cnt}, \texttt{amp\_cnt}, \texttt{query\_len}) carry mean $|$SHAP$|$ identically zero in every run of both families, so they occupy the same tie in both profiles and agree by construction. Recomputed over the 16 features that carry any attribution at all, the correlations fall by roughly $0.04$ (CatBoost $0.932\pm0.041$ against $0.944$ and $0.933$; XGBoost $0.929\pm0.035$ against $0.907$ and $0.965$); the comparison to the null reaches the same verdict on the same families.%
