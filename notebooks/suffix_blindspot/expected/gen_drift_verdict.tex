Applied forward over 6 equal-count time windows, the early-window model's recall on freshly-arrived phishing falls from $0.603$ to $0.475$, and the attribution profile moves with it: over the windows, recall's Spearman correlation with $|$SHAP$|$ is $-0.77$ for \texttt{dot\_cnt} and $-0.71$ for \texttt{dash\_cnt} (both rise as recall falls) and $+0.71$ for \texttt{tld\_len} (the dominant prior weakens). Two-sided reference $p$-values from all $6! = 720$ pairings are $0.103$, $0.136$ and $0.136$ respectively. None reaches $p<0.05$ even under the exchangeability assumption; temporal dependence between windows is not accounted for by this reference test. With only 6 windows this is a descriptive co-movement, not a powered lead/lag test, and we report it as one: the monitoring candidate is neither refuted nor established, and what it earns is a powered replication in a study designed for it rather than a place among this paper's findings%
